What it is

Describe the widgets you want to ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or any AI assistant, and it writes the layout: which apps, in what order, in which sections, with what look. Iconic imports the answer. It is a Pro feature, and Pro is free during early access.

How to use it

  1. In Iconic, open the menu (top left), tap Build with AI, then Copy my setup. This copies your widgets, their sizes and your installed apps, with instructions for the AI.
  2. Paste it into your AI and say what you want.
  3. Copy the AI's answer and tap Import from clipboard in Iconic, or share the answer to Iconic.

A new layout is saved and you can put it on a widget right away. If the AI changed one of your existing widgets (by its widgetId), Iconic asks before replacing it.

You can also open or share a .json layout file to Iconic.

Privacy

Your setup only leaves your phone when you paste it somewhere yourself. Iconic itself sends nothing.

Starting from this website

If you want to start in your AI first, paste this prompt. Copy my setup in the app is better, because it includes your real app list and widget sizes.

I use Iconic, an Android app that shows small app icons in home-screen widgets.
Write me a widget layout in the format documented at
https://0x2alx.github.io/iconic-site/ai.html
Ask me what I want first. Then answer with one JSON code block.

Without your app list, the AI has to guess package names, and a wrong guess won't open anything.

Format reference

A machine-readable JSON Schema (draft 2020-12) describes the same format.

Top level

  • One layout: {"name": "Media", "items": [...], ...settings}
  • Several layouts: {"configs": [layout, layout]}. A plain list of layouts, [layout, layout], also works, and so does a backup file made by Back up to file.
  • widgetId: optional. Give the id of one of your widgets (listed in Copy my setup) to change it in place. The layout replaces the widget's whole setup (nothing is merged), so give its complete new item list; Iconic asks before replacing it. Leave it out, or use 0, and the layout is saved and you pick where it goes.

Items

items lists what the widget shows, in display order. Each item has a type:

  • {"type": "app", "packageName": "com.netflix.mediaclient", "label": "Netflix"}: an app. packageName and label are required. Iconic finds the launcher activity itself, so activityName can be left out.
  • {"type": "section", "label": "🎬 Watch"}: starts a titled group of icons. Emoji work in titles. A blank label ("label": "") draws a thin divider instead. Optional sameRow and width place it in a grid, and an optional style changes this section only.
  • {"type": "link", "label": "News", "url": "https://example.com"}: a web address, shown as a letter tile. label and url are required.

Shortcuts (a contact, a chat) can't be written by hand. Add those in the app.

Custom icons

Apps use their own icon and links a letter tile, unless you give them a picture. This is for AI agents that can download an image and look at it, such as coding agents with web access. Most chat apps can't, so leave icons out there.

  • On the app or link, add "icon": {"type": "image", "file": "reddit"}. The file is any name you choose.
  • Next to items (or next to configs), add an icons map from that name to the image as base64: "icons": {"reddit": "iVBORw0KGgo..."}. PNG, JPEG and WebP work, and so does a data:image/png;base64, address.
  • Use a square image, about 256 px, with its own background colour. It fills the whole icon shape, like the app icons next to it. Iconic crops anything else to a square and scales it to 256 px.
  • Check the image is the right logo before you use it. Iconic never downloads anything itself.

Settings

All settings are optional. A setting that is left out, or has a value Iconic can't read, takes its default. Names of types and values ignore case, so "Squircle" works, but write them as shown.

Section grid

By default sections stack, one under another. Two fields on a section item build a grid instead. A widget has at most 12 sections.

FieldTypeDefaultMeaning
sameRowtrue or falsefalseTrue puts the section to the right of the one before it, instead of below. Rows of side-by-side sections make a grid.
widthnumber11 to 6. The section's share of its row. Two sections with widths 1 and 2 get a third and two thirds.

The widget is laid out as a grid when any section has sameRow, or has its own iconSizeDp or layout in its style. Otherwise it keeps the classic stacked flow. Sections in a grid can't scroll: overflow only applies to the whole widget. Several rows of side-by-side sections are a Pro feature (free for now); stacked sections, or one row of side-by-side sections, are free.

{"type": "section", "label": "🎬 Watch", "style": {"colorArgb": "#FF5252", "sizeSp": 12, "bold": true, "font": "serif", "align": "start", "gapXDp": 2, "gapYDp": 6, "iconSizeDp": 48, "shape": "circle"}}

Colours are written as "#RRGGBB" or "#AARRGGBB" (alpha first), like "#E53935".

Numbers outside their range are brought into it.

What the importer forgives: the JSON can sit inside a code fence or among other text, as AI answers usually do. Trailing commas and comments are allowed. An item or layout that can't be read is skipped and the rest still imports.

The app theme

The look of the Iconic app itself (its screens, never the widgets) can come from an AI too. Put "theme" next to a layout, {"items": [...], "theme": {...}} or {"configs": [...], "theme": {...}}, or answer {"theme": {...}} alone.

{"theme": {"selectedId": "my-theme", "custom": [{"id": "my-theme", "name": "Ocean", "dark": true,
  "background": "#0B1A2A", "surface": "#13263B", "surfaceVariant": "#1C334D", "border": "#27425F",
  "accent": "#3FC1C9", "onAccent": "#062026", "text": "#E6F1F8", "mutedText": "#9DB4C7", "danger": "#FF6B6B",
  "headingFont": "audiowide", "bodyFont": "default", "textScalePct": 100, "cornerDp": 14, "borderDp": 1}]}}

Built-in ids: graphite (the default), daylight, neon, signature, high-contrast. Fonts: default, condensed, serif, monospace, audiowide, oxanium, orbitron, michroma, tektur, rajdhani, chakraPetch, shareTechMono. Limits: textScalePct 85 to 130, cornerDp 0 to 28, borderDp 0 to 3. Text must stay readable against its background: Iconic still saves a theme that isn't, says which text is hard to read, and its theme editor offers the nearest colours that pass.

Sizing guide

For the auto layout, work out how many icons fit before choosing the icon size and the number of apps. All sizes are in dp (labels and titles are in sp, counted the same here).

  • Columns: floor((width + gap) / (icon + gap))
  • Height: icon rows × (icon + label) + titles + one gap between every two lines (rows and titles). A section's own gapYDp replaces the gap between its rows, and its gapXDp the gap between its icons (so its column count too). Gaps around titles stay gapDp.
  • Label: sizeSp × 1.3 rounded down, + 2, when labels show, otherwise 0.
  • Section title: title sizeSp × 1.3 rounded down, + 6 (11 sp makes a 20 dp title). A divider is 9.
  • Grid: each section gets a rectangle. Its width is (widget width − sectionGapDp between sections) × its width share. Its columns = floor((width + gap) / (icon + gap)), then balanced (6 apps in 5 columns show as 3 + 3). A row is as tall as its tallest section. The rows plus sectionGapDp between them must fit the height, otherwise every section's icons shrink together. Sections with their own iconSizeDp keep it.

When it doesn't fit, icons shrink 1 dp at a time, down to 16 dp. For big icons, use fewer apps, titles or labels.

Tip: keep each section's app count a multiple of the column count, so every row is full.

Worked example: a widget 80 × 261 dp, gap 4, no labels. Columns at 38 dp icons: floor(84 / 42) = 2. At 40 dp it would be floor(84 / 44) = 1, so 38 is the largest size for 2 columns. Two titled sections of 6 and 4 apps make 3 + 2 = 5 icon rows, so the height is 5 × 38 = 190, plus two titles of 20 (40), plus 6 gaps of 4 (24): 254 dp, which fits in 261.
